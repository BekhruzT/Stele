**The intended listening experience.**

An awake listener should have a concrete question to follow. A drifting listener should be able to return to a person, place, or problem that still makes sense. Information earns its place by changing the listener's understanding of that question. Names and measurements support the story rather than determine its pace.

The voice is conversational and measured. Short sentences can land an observation; longer ones can carry a continuous thought. No sentence-length quota, mandatory promise inventory, or periodic atmosphere paragraph was imposed. Historical scene details are taken from accounts rather than invented to make a room feel cinematic. Hypothetical examples are explicitly introduced as examples.

**Meitner: the editorial choices.**

The central question is why a radioactive product of uranium behaved like a much lighter element, and how an exiled member of the original research team helped explain it. The opening now uses the user's chosen hook: “The discovery that opened the nuclear age started with an accident.” The existing prose follows unchanged, with the letter in the next paragraph. Its puzzle is understandable before the biography begins. The hook establishes significance and leaves the discovery to unfold in the narration.

The opening previews the result deliberately. Returning to the letter later adds the control experiment and Meitner's response; it is not a second cold open. The emotional thread is a working relationship sustained across separation, with its achievements and later disagreements left intact.

Details retained include the separate basement entrance, contamination precautions, the cosmic/cosmetic misprint, the ring and small amount of cash, the walk recalled by Frisch, and the notebook questions. Each does a different job. The script omits most appointment dates, intermediary officials, reagent weights, birth-date disputes, medal administration, graphic wartime descriptions, and funeral details.

The new script also corrects several misleading directions in the earlier material:

- Meitner and Frisch explain an observed fission process; she does not devise an operating method for splitting atoms with a pencil.
- The chemical evidence belongs to Hahn and Strassmann. The first chemistry paper is not described as a single-author paper.
- The barium carrier is explained. Otherwise, the listener could reasonably wonder why finding a substance the chemists had deliberately added proved anything.
- The Christmas scene is attributed to Frisch's later recollection, without an unsupported exact calendar day.
- The explanation distinguishes the nuclear model from literal liquid water and accounts for energy through the change in rest mass and binding.
- The private collaboration continued after exile. Meitner is not depicted as a stranger who solved a problem in one isolated moment.
- The later life includes renewed research support in Sweden and her move to Cambridge. Her story is not reduced to losing a prize.

**Before/after examples that can guide later pipeline work.**

| Earlier tendency | Candidate treatment | Principle demonstrated |
|---|---|---|
| Biography or conference chronology before the subject is clear | Meitner's letter and Bell's measurement question appear immediately | Establish a reason to listen before introducing a history |
| Exact quantities standing in for an explanation | The known radium separates; the unknown product follows barium | Explain what the comparison establishes |
| “Worked out how to split the uranium atom, with a pencil” | A collaborative interpretation of chemical evidence, followed by a physical test | Make the accurate claim interesting |
| “The moon exists when nobody looks at it” | Local explanations, independent settings, and a statistical limit | Preserve the assumptions that give a result its meaning |
| Generic restarts at every segment boundary | A transition follows a question the preceding paragraphs created | Carry understanding across the boundary |
| Repeated broad conclusions and object-based closing templates | One documented late-life notebook, then a short close | Let an established detail finish the story |

These are examples of editorial judgment, not new universal quotas to put into a prompt.
