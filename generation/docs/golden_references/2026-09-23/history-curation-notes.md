# History candidate references

These passages were composed directly for the history exercise on 23 September 2026. They have not
been generated by the pipeline or approved by the user as final prose. Their status is a candidate
golden set. Earlier science references and approved hooks remain unchanged.

## What applies already, and what was missing

History and science use the same planner, separate hook selection, whole-narrative writer, reviewer,
repair loop, and short introductions for people. Previously both received Meitner and Bell style
excerpts. Harrison was the only new-topic history sample tested in the earlier exercise, and it was
still a story of technical investigation. That left ordinary historical narration under-tested.

The two new references test different work:

- **Silk Roads:** a complete short narrative, 1,779 words, explaining trade through correspondence,
  practical obligations, and the distinction between moving goods and people being able to move.
  It is a selected story through fourth-century letters, not a survey of every era of Silk Road trade.
- **Mansa Musa:** a connected opening sample, 1,187 words, moving from a reported court encounter to
  gold prices and the practical arrangements described by a later traveller. It is not a complete
  history of Mali, Songhai, or medieval African trade.

The new hooks are editorial proposals. They have not been added to the user-approved hook registry.
History generation receives excerpts from these two files through `style-reference-catalog.json`.
Science continues to receive the same Meitner and Bell passages as before. Exact excerpts and full
source-file hashes are recorded with every run.

## Corrections to the legacy outlines

The comparison input is `generation/tools/history_reference_plan.json`. It is a bounded, checked
replacement for the relevant legacy outlines in these runs; the broad original outlines were not
silently relabelled as verified.

- Nanai-vandak writes to Varzakk in letter 2. Nanai-dhat is Miwnay's husband in other letters. The
  old outline conflates them and reverses the direction of the missing news.
- “No merchant ever walked the entire route,” universal day-spaced inns, a monopoly over freight,
  and an international Sogdian coin standard are not established by these sources.
- A writer's report of disrupted cities is not direct evidence that every market vanished overnight.
- Musa's precise entourage totals and modern wealth rankings are not used. Al-Umari's explanation
  of the gold price change remains attributed instead of becoming a proved single-cause crash.
- Ibn Battuta's 1352 caravan is distinguished from Musa's 1324 visit. Its salt mine is Taghaza;
  later Taudeni is not inserted into the episode. Reported prices per load do not mean equal weights
  of salt and gold traded everywhere.
- The remaining, broad legacy claims about plague transmission, papermaking, Songhai, Timbuktu's
  manuscript totals, and Great Zimbabwe are outside these selected stories and are not certified.

## Editorial lessons added to the pipeline

1. Explain what a person is trying to do and the practical or institutional constraint they face.
   A messenger requesting water makes cooperation intelligible without a paragraph defining trust.
2. Introduce a person by their relevant role or relationship, and let an institution acquire meaning
   through what it does. Names are not proof of specificity.
3. Establish a source's perspective once where it changes the listener's reading of an event.
   Keep later qualifications local; avoid repeating a history-method lecture.
4. Mark time and place shifts. A later episode can illuminate a problem without becoming eyewitness
   evidence for an earlier one. Do not assemble a timeless civilization from convenient details.
5. Give supporting people agency, including settled contacts, hosts, labourers, and messengers.
   Preserve coercion where relevant without graphic detours or turning every ending into a moral.
6. Keep the hooks concise and exempt from mandatory role labels. The body supplies needed context.

These rules are applied to history only through `HISTORY_GUIDANCE`; shared prose principles remain
shared. Scientific meaning is reviewed when the history concerns a technical mechanism, and is not
applicable for purely historical claims. Source fidelity always remains applicable.

No listening test or complete primary-source audit has been performed. These short references
establish editorial examples, not evidence that a 90-minute narration will sustain the same quality.
