# What to transfer from the 45 openings

These are editorial observations, not measured claims about retention. The pool
is mostly educational science, with only three history-of-science examples. It
does not replace research on the original sleep-history channels. Each quotation
was checked against its cached community transcript, not independently against
the video audio.

1. **Give scale a familiar reference.** The giant organism is too wide to hug,
   while surrounding plants reach your ankles (YT002). One exploding star can
   outshine a galaxy (YT014). Seven neck bones means something because a giraffe
   and a human share the count (YT031). The number is useful because of the
   relationship, not because it sounds large.
2. **Explain the normal expectation before the exception.** Wolves still exist
   after dogs were domesticated; where are the ancestors of cattle (YT030)?
   Spiders are predators; this one mostly eats plants (YT041). An unsupported
   expectation cannot be repaired with the word 'but'.
3. **Let a concrete consequence carry significance.** Dust can turn day into
   night on Mars (YT015); microbes can dissolve protected stone (YT003). A list
   of interesting properties, or a sentence saying a discovery was important,
   does not do the same work.
4. **A familiar object can open a hidden world.** A drink becomes an ecosystem
   (YT024); rocks become evidence about planets and life (YT005); a storage
   problem on cloudy days leads to rust (YT028). This pattern only fits when
   the object's role is central to the promised video. Do not replace a story
   about superconductivity with an incidental flask anecdote.
5. **A premise and a turn may need two sentences.** Do not mechanically imitate
   each source's first sentence. It can be a greeting, a joke, or incomplete
   setup. The quote field preserves the shortest selected complete passage;
   source_first_sentence and skipped_prefix make its position explicit. Our
   target is a much shorter opening, generally one or two clear sentences.
6. **Reveal the discovery without explaining its mechanism.** The point of a
   liquid mirror is clear before the optics lesson (YT009). The prospect of
   getting energy without sunlight is clear before the chemistry (YT040).
   Withholding the subject produces confusion, not useful suspense.
7. **Curiosity need not be peril.** The red pigment's combination of useful
   properties (YT001), a pink lake (YT020), and an ecosystem in a glass
   (YT024) create questions without threat or self-praise. Bedtime narration
   should transfer the idea while dropping shouted reactions and gross-out jokes.
8. **Preserve the small words that make a claim true.** 'Might' matters for a
   space-travel application (YT045); 'mostly' matters for the spider's diet
   (YT041). A title may be more categorical than the narration. Do not train
   on the title as though it were the spoken opening or established evidence.

## What the live trials taught us

The first trial reproduced known rejected patterns despite all 45 examples being
present: an unexplained sixty-mile error; delayed Silk Road correspondence; a
chemist's biographical situation instead of the significance of fission; and a
flask invention instead of superconductivity. The model's selector passed all
six. That is evidence of a selection failure, not six good hooks.

The calibrated prompt retains all 45 examples but makes the user's negative
calibration explicit. It also permits five constructions of the strongest
premise, instead of encouraging five unrelated body anecdotes. No literal
approved answer for these six topics is included in fresh hook calls. The
approved registry still protects production wording on the four approved topics.

Read `six-case-review.json` and `generations/hook-research.html` for the actual
second-run verdicts. Both runs retain their raw requests, outputs, corpus hashes,
and model usage. This is a comparison of combined prompt changes, not an isolated
or statistically reliable estimate of adding examples. Full bodies were not
regenerated in this hook-only experiment.

## Subsequent prompt reevaluation

See [the September 24 follow-up](prompt-reevaluation.md). The active writer now
produces only spoken hooks; source annotation and listener judgment are separate.
All 45 quotations remain. Explicit user rejections are enforced in the listener
prompt. The latest review separates assistant picks from model-selected wording,
and does not equate a model pass with a successful hook.
